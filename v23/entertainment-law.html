Common questions

Do I need to register my copyright?

Your work is protected the moment it is fixed, written down or recorded. In the United States, however, a work generally must be registered before you can sue for infringement, and registering promptly preserves the right to seek statutory damages and attorney’s fees. Registration is inexpensive insurance for work that matters.

Should I sign the contract I’ve been sent?

Not before it is reviewed. Recording, publishing, management, production and distribution agreements often decide who owns the work, for how long and where, how costs are recouped before you are paid, and what you can never do again. Those terms are far easier to change before you sign than after.

Someone is using my work without permission. What now?

Keep copies, links and dates, and avoid public accusations. Depending on the facts, the remedy may be a takedown notice, a letter, a negotiated license, or a lawsuit. Mr. Starke chose law after a song he co-wrote was taken without credit, so this is the work that started his career.
