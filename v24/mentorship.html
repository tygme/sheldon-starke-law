Teaching & mentorship

Passing on what he knows

For more than 35 years Mr. Starke has shared his knowledge freely: in lecture halls and on the radio, in print and on television, and one client at a time.

In the classroom

Mr. Starke has lectured on entertainment law and related subjects at ten universities, colleges and conservatories in Ohio, and for the profession itself.

  • Case Western Reserve University
  • Cleveland State University
  • Baldwin Wallace University
  • Kent State University
  • Oberlin College
  • University of Akron
  • Cleveland Institute of Music
  • Cleveland Institute of Art
  • Cuyahoga Community College
  • Lakeland Community College

He has also spoken for the Ohio State Bar Association, the Cleveland Law Library, several federal agencies, and the industry publications DJ Times and Mobile Beat, and he taught peace officers in Ohio Peace Officer Training courses.

On the air and in print

For more than five years he hosted weekly entertainment law segments on WWWE and WERE, and he has been a guest on WNCX, WMMS and many other stations. He has published more than 300 articles and scripts across television, radio, video, newspapers and magazines, and produced a television series that taught entertainment law to a general audience.

Building a creative community

Mr. Starke twice brought the Songwriters Guild of America, then the American Guild of Authors and Composers, to Cleveland: the city’s first major initiative to bring music education to the public. His office arranged for Guild President George David Weiss to receive the Key to the City.

In 1989 he created the Entertainment Business Expo: 118 courses over three days, with three to five sessions running at once, on music, film, television, radio, video and media, drawing industry leaders from around the world.

A mentor to his clients

Much of his mentoring happens one person at a time. Artists starting out, professionals facing their first board inquiry, employees confronting their employer: clients describe a lawyer who explains, teaches and checks in long after the work is done.
