About

Sheldon Philip Starke

A Cleveland native who passed three bar examinations, tried murder cases and landmark civil matters, and built one of the Midwest’s most established entertainment law practices.

Sheldon P. Starke has dedicated his career to advancing justice and advocating for his clients across a wide range of legal fields. He passed three separate bar examinations, in Ohio, Florida and New York, and is admitted to practice before the Supreme Court of the United States, the U.S. Court of Military Appeals, and multiple federal courts.

Early career

Before becoming an attorney, Mr. Starke interned with the City of Cleveland Law Department. He then became the first law intern in the United States to be trained in a sheriff’s office, serving with the Cuyahoga County Sheriff’s Office. After graduation he served as Executive Assistant to the Sheriff of Cuyahoga County, working across every criminal and civil department, and was trained by Maj. Louis Kulis, regarded as the “Sherlock Holmes” of his time.

He lectured at Ohio Peace Officer Training courses and, at the request of the Mayor, was appointed temporary prosecutor for Brook Park, Ohio. In the 1990s he assisted British police and Scotland Yard with a series of unexplained crimes, which ceased after his involvement and action by the authorities.

Trial work and representative matters

His criminal work includes murder trials, among them the longest murder trial in the history of Naples, Collier County, Florida. His civil work has repeatedly made new law.

  • State of Florida v. PahlCollier County, Florida

    Laid the groundwork for abolishing Florida’s Dead Man’s Statute.

  • United States v. WilliamsU.S. Court of Appeals, Sixth Circuit

    Federal criminal conspiracy defense.

  • Today’s Headlines v. AbelCuyahoga County Common Pleas, Judge James McMonagle

    Won a published opinion on trademarks and the Deceptive Trade Practices Act, a rare recognition for a Common Pleas court.

  • Katz v. Metro TrafficCV 93 24699, Cuyahoga County

    Filed the first spoliation (destruction of evidence) claim before the Ohio Supreme Court formally recognized it.

  • Thomas v. Schwartz FursOhio

    Consumer protection case on four separate claims that created new law in Ohio.

  • American Embassy Association v. TeweldeEEOC No. 100-98-0368

    As U.S. Special Counsel for the Bonn “spy embassy” wind-up, defended the Association; the EEOC complaint was dismissed by the Acting Director.

  • Senior OSHA staff v. United StatesAge discrimination class action

    Represented all senior members of OSHA in the largest age discrimination class action of its kind against the federal government.

Federal practice

Mr. Starke has represented federal employees in cases against multiple agencies, including all senior members of OSHA in the largest age discrimination class action of its kind against the U.S. government. He has handled well over one hundred mediations at the EEOC and other venues, with a success rate above 95 percent.

Entertainment law

He has published more than 300 articles and scripts across television, radio, video, newspapers and magazines. For more than five years he hosted weekly entertainment law segments on WWWE and WERE, and he has appeared on WNCX, WMMS and many other stations. He has licensed or negotiated rights for about one hundred films and videos, and has represented or negotiated for Movies of the Week, publishers, record companies, distributors, producers and broadcasters worldwide.

Variety recognized him as a lawyer for theater owners. He brought the American Guild of Authors and Composers (later the Songwriters Guild of America) to Cleveland, the city’s first major initiative in public music education, and his office arranged for Guild President George David Weiss to receive the Key to the City. In 1989 he created the Entertainment Business Expo: 118 courses over three days on music, film, television, radio, video and media, drawing industry leaders from around the world.

Corporate and international work

In 1983 Mr. Starke became General Counsel of Enter-Tel, Inc., negotiating international contracts for movies, cartoons and series, and playing a role in producing The Quincy Jones Show and the award-winning series America’s Best.

The American Embassy Association later selected him as U.S. Special Counsel to wind up the Bonn embassy property in Germany, the so-called “spy embassy” created after World War II. The matter raised significant constitutional questions about applying U.S. law to foreign embassy employees, and he successfully defended the Association in The American Embassy Association v. Tewelde.

Public service

Public service runs through Mr. Starke’s career from its first day: in the Sheriff’s Office, as a prosecutor, as an instructor of peace officers, and in his work with British authorities. In the arts he gave Cleveland its first major public music-education initiative and an international entertainment business conference.
