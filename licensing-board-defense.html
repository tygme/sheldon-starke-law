Professional licensing defense · Ohio & Florida

Protect the license you spent a career earning

A complaint or investigation by a licensing board can change a professional life. Experienced counsel from the first contact protects your credentials, your career and your reputation.

First, the most important advice

Never return an initial phone call from a licensing board without counsel.

Calls to boards may be recorded, and even well-intentioned answers can be used against you.

Why early advice matters

A professional who receives notice from a state licensing board should not handle it alone. Legal counsel places a protective barrier between you and the board. Even before formal notice arrives, if you believe your position is threatened or your job is at risk, speak with an attorney. The earlier the start, the stronger the defense.

Retaining counsel is not an admission

Some professionals worry that hiring a lawyer will make them look guilty. Boards deal with counsel every day. Having an experienced attorney involved shows that you take your rights and your career seriously, and it usually makes the process clearer and more respectful.

Mr. Starke’s approach emphasizes discretion, strong advocacy and practical solutions that protect both the license and the person who holds it.

Common questions

A board investigator called me. Should I call back?

Not before speaking with counsel. Investigators are gathering evidence, calls may be recorded, and well-meant explanations can be used against you. Your lawyer can make the contact on your behalf.

Can my license be affected before there is a hearing?

In serious cases some boards can suspend a license on an emergency basis before a full hearing. That is one more reason to get advice at the first sign of an inquiry, not after a formal notice arrives.

What outcomes are possible?

Many matters close with no action or a warning. Others resolve through a negotiated agreement, sometimes with conditions such as education or monitoring. Suspension and revocation are the most serious outcomes, and early, careful preparation is the best way to avoid them.

Will my employer find out?

That depends on the profession, the board, your employer’s policies and the nature of the complaint. It is one of the first questions to raise with counsel, before you say anything to anyone.
